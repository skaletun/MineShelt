# Interiors, habitation and gameplay

A Minecraft building is also a traversable play space. A beautiful facade does not excuse an inaccessible loft, fake staircase, clipped bed, unlit route or invisible gameplay affordance.

Before adding deep architectural reveals, balconies, protruding piers, eaves and porticos, reconcile exterior relief with clear usable interior depth and real door/stair/collision space. See [architectural-depth-and-relief.md](architectural-depth-and-relief.md).

## The occupancy program

Before the build, name occupants, use zones and expected actions:
- arrival and storage;
- work and social zones;
- private sleeping/living areas;
- machinery, heating or ritual areas;
- vertical circulation;
- safe emergency/secondary access where required by the brief;
- hidden adventure routes, loot or encounter spaces when relevant.

Translate activities into real block-space. A cramped cottage can be charming; a blacksmith cannot forge if the room has no clearance around the hearth.

## Interior architecture first

Resolve the interior shell before furniture:
1. Clear heights and structural supports.
2. Doorways and line-of-sight.
3. Stairs, ladders, rails or portals and their entrance/exit clearance.
4. Window position, daylight and nighttime lighting.
5. Usable floor area after furnishing.
6. Furnishing driven by the building's role.

A small room can have one expressive corner and several quiet surfaces. Filling every spare block with barrels, pots and chains makes a stage set, not a lived-in interior.

## Traversal test

If tools are available, actually walk the structure (or use a reliable collision/path test) and answer:
- Can an ordinary player enter without breaking anything?
- Can the player reach stairs and each intended upper floor?
- Can doors, trapdoors and gates open in their real game state?
- Does intended mob navigation work where it matters?
- Are ceiling lanterns, signs and beams clear of movement?
- Can ladders be mounted, dismounted and exited?
- Are cliff-edge entrances, bridges and balconies safe according to brief?
- Do furnishings block crafting, containers, workstation or interactive block access?

A plan view alone does not validate a usable staircase.

## Lighting and atmosphere

Use daylight where activities need it; supplement with plausible light sources. Check nighttime/spawn safety as required by edition, difficulty and gamerules. Don't assert absolute spawn proof without a game-specific calculation/test.

Avoid evenly distributed lantern grids unless the building is deliberately institutional. A forge lights its workbench, furnace and threshold; a chapel lights an altar, procession path and wall niches.

Atmosphere should result from space: framed views, ceiling rhythm, areas of compression and release, material warmth, shadows and a strong focal point.

## Furnishing through implied life

Choose objects based on use and wear:
- a baker has a heat-safe oven zone, flour/food storage, worktable and sales/front delivery path;
- a dock warehouse needs broad doors, moving routes, shelves and covered loading;
- a tower needs sightline, messenger signals, stairs and basic guard equipment;
- a ruined shrine may show a blocked aisle, lost roof and shifted ceremonial center.

Avoid unrelated loot displays and repeated barrels placed only to fill a corner.

## Build for gameplay roles

If the building hosts a quest, NPC, loot chest, crafting station or dungeon:
- identify interactive blocks and their required space;
- mark access paths and player guidance;
- design combat sightlines, recovery areas and escape routes when necessary;
- coordinate light, spawns and atmosphere;
- separate decorative fake doors from real progression routes and make this distinction readable.

Do not invent mod-specific interactions. Check actual game data and interfaces.

## Last-pass interior photo set

Document entrance looking in, each floor's primary space, stair exit, critical gameplay station, night conditions and any player collision issue. If no runtime is accessible, mark the interior as geometrically planned but unverified for movement.
