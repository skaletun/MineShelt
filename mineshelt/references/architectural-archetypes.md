# Architectural archetypes — study, never stamp

An archetype is a family of **causal architectural ideas**, not a reusable prefab. Adapt its form to inhabitants, era, terrain, climate and site. Every commission should make at least one specific choice that would *not* work in another context.

## Domestic: cottage, manor, longhouse

**Key question:** how do occupants enter, work, store goods and sleep? Start with lived spatial sequencing. A fishing cottage needs net-drying and boat access; a forest ranger's refuge needs covered wood, an observation window and a safe entrance.

- Cottage: compact hierarchy, practical roof, one sheltered threshold.
- Longhouse: longitudinal rhythm tied to real beams and activities, identifiable communal hearth.
- Manor: multiple volumes justified by private/public hierarchy and a more formal approach.

Avoid putting oversized chimneys, dormers and balconies on every home.

## Productive: smithy, mill, bakery, warehouse

Let work define geometry.
- Forge: heat-resistant core, clear ventilation, heavy-working clearance, fuel and cart handling.
- Watermill: physically legible intake/wheel/shaft relation and downstream flow; do not float a wheel in empty air.
- Bakery: oven mass, deliveries, sale/service division.
- Warehouse: broad loading doors, strong spans, storage height and dock/road connection.

Functional evidence should be visible even without a sign.

## Civic: inn, guildhall, market, watchpost

These buildings organize public space.
- Inn: accessible approach, social interior, service access, lodging separation.
- Guildhall: symbolic public front and gathering volume.
- Market: open circulation, shade/weather protection, vendor bays.
- Watchpost: actual sightlines, protected ascent and visible signaling points.

The most important elevation is usually the approach-facing one.

## Sacred/magical: shrine, observatory, archive

A strong ceremonial build has a legible spatial sequence and a controlled climax. Symmetry can be meaningful, but shouldn't force a nonsensical terrain cut. An observatory's geometry should acknowledge sightlines, roof movement or a cosmic framing device. A magical floating structure needs a deliberately communicated suspension system.

Avoid using purple glass, chains, candles and floating shards as interchangeable shorthand for “magic.”

## Defense: gatehouse, tower, fort, ruin

Security creates form: defensible entrances, access height, observation and wall/ground relationships. Wall thickness should support walkways if walkways are intended. A watchtower's silhouette is different from a palace spire because of function. A ruin needs readable original structure and plausible chronological damage.

Avoid unreachable battlements and fortresses with large undefended ground-floor glass walls unless narrative justifies them.

## Regional languages

### Cold conifer biome
Covered entrances, timber assembly and stone footings; strong storm response; compact protected yard. Don't equate “cold” with mandatory exaggerated spires.

### Dry desert/mesa
Sunshade, thermal mass, courtyards and deep openings; dust exposure and regional earthen/stone color. Not every building needs the same flat-top silhouette.

### Coast/wetland
Elevation, drainage, docks, storm-protected roofs, salt-worn surfaces and wind exposure. Routes over water are as important as facade detail.

### Mountains
Anchoring, vertical entry changes, retaining structures, compact windward face and dramatic landscape silhouette. Architecture should embrace rock rather than hovering above it.

### Dense town
Plot boundaries, connected roofs with careful junctions, active ground floors and service alleys. Neighborhood coherence is compatible with real formal diversity.

## Avoid aesthetic clichés

Replace “make it medieval” with a coherent local construction culture: stone plinths, framed plaster, local timber, repair conventions, roof pitch range and window typology. Then let a wealthy guildhall, an old tavern and a working barn express those conventions differently.

Replace “make it more detailed” with a concrete critique: add a genuinely sheltered entry, resolve one blank gable, articulate structural bays or expose a meaningful work zone.

Replace “make it organic” with motivated departures from perfect alignment. Randomly varying dimensions is not history.

## Originality exercise

Before building:
1. Identify one strong precedent or archetype.
2. Name its defining causal rule.
3. Name the local constraint that modifies that rule.
4. Show the resulting **visible design decision**.
5. Ensure the design still reads when viewed as a silhouette.

This method creates differences that humans can recognize as intentional.
