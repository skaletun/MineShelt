# Vegetation, planting ecology and hand-built custom trees

An environment looks handmade when plants have **ecological placement, species identity, size hierarchy, silhouette and spaces between them**. Do not solve landscaping by scattering bonemeal growth, leaves and flowers evenly.

## 1. Site-specific planting plan

Before placing a tree, classify parts of the site:
- dry/exposed ridges and outcrops;
- sheltered gullies and wet soils;
- path and settlement edges;
- cultivated ground and garden boundaries;
- dark forest interior, woodland clearings and shores;
- places where views to important architecture must remain open.

Choose a distinct population plan: large canopy landmarks, medium trees, juveniles, shrubs, groundcover and intentional clearings. Tree positions should explain both natural growth and human maintenance.

**Open space is an ingredient.** A house can't be appreciated if every elevation is hidden by identical leaf blobs.

## 2. Make an individual tree rather than an inflated vanilla tree

### Trunk and character

Determine species and growth conditions. A mountain pine may have a thick asymmetric wind-shaped trunk; a sheltered oak may have a wide crown and broad lateral branches. Begin by marking the tree's total silhouette and canopy occupancy.

Add taper and directional lean in deliberate sections. For old trees, buttress or expose roots around soil and rock; roots should visually anchor the trunk. Do not place perfectly vertical same-width stems everywhere unless constructing a planted orchard.

### Branching

Create a recognizable branching hierarchy:
1. primary trunk from visible root flare;
2. two or a few defining large branches that establish crown direction;
3. intermediate forks that extend the intended crown;
4. narrow tip branches supporting leaves.

Avoid branches evenly spaced like a spiral staircase or disconnected logs embedded in foliage. From below, branch paths should be reasonably traceable back to the trunk.

### Crown / leaf volumes

Create distinct leaf masses separated by negative-space gaps. Instead of a single sphere, use crown lobes that correspond to branch structure. Preserve some sky holes and irregular but purposeful edges.

Leaves shouldn't form a uniform cube, cone or identical ball unless the species, growth habit or deliberate topiary calls for it. Check leaf decay/distance/persistence for edited trees, especially after save/load or leaf updates.

### Species sketches

| Species idea | Structure | Crown and details | Frequent mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old spruce | tapered, tall central trunk; sloping branches | layered downward boughs with gaps and widening then tapering silhouette | perfect repeated conical leaf tiers |
| Weather-beaten pine | offset/bent trunk with few major forks | sparse high crown, wind-swept one side | symmetrical umbrella crown on exposed ridge |
| Valley oak | heavy trunk and 3–5 major lateral limbs | broad lobed crown, space under canopy | sphere of leaves atop a pole |
| Birch grove | lighter slimmer trunks, clustered population | broken higher crowns, varied ages | equal-height white sticks on grid |
| Willow-like stream tree | low spreading support limbs | hanging foliage near banks and water | solid featureless green curtain |
| Orchard tree | practical short trunk and managed crown | open walkways and intentional fruiting canopy | random wild old-growth scale in a farm row |

Only suggest blocks compatible with actual Minecraft/mod; e.g. a “willow-like” shape may use accessible vanilla leaves, not a guessed willow mod ID.

## 3. Tree scale relates to architecture

A 40-block giant pine can be excellent in a mountain panorama but hide a 10-block cottage entirely. Use composition:
- foreground framing tree whose branches partially frame—not conceal—the entry;
- middle ground smaller trees that link yard to forest;
- distant tall trees that articulate the ridge;
- at least one deliberate gap for the hero view.

Vary ages and species because the site supports it, not through a random height sampler.

## 4. Undergrowth as ecological clusters

Grass, flowers, ferns, bushes and roots concentrate according to microclimate:
- around tree root zones: lower light, leaf litter, podzol/rooted dirt and shade-tolerant growth;
- under eaves: drier or wetter depending on runoff;
- at stream bank: dense riparian planting, with openings at access points;
- along walked trail: compressed/bare middle, vegetated verge and disturbed corners;
- on rocky ledges: sparse shrubs in soil pockets;
- cultivated garden: spacing and paths reflect maintenance.

Leave maintenance strips near walls and doors where people regularly walk. A path lost under ferns is a failed inhabited site.

## 5. Rock and root relationships

Position boulders as rock fragments with plausible buried mass. A boulder half sunk into the grade reads as heavier than a freestanding 2×2 stone lump. Roots can wrap around or penetrate natural seams, but don't randomly spawn logs through masonry.

Use a group of rocks with a clear larger/smaller hierarchy and a few open gaps. Not every cluster needs mushrooms, moss, cobwebs and dripping vines.

## 6. Human modifications

Garden beds, orchards, hedges, woodlots and planted avenue trees often follow human geometry:
- rows support harvest or civic ceremony;
- paths define social approach;
- managed pollards/pruning creates visible purpose;
- old walls can host volunteer plants at cracks and drainage points;
- repair or clearing near structures can reveal settlement history.

It is okay for an orchard to be regular. **“Organic” is not an excuse to make all built landscapes crooked.**

## 7. Built tree acceptance

Check five views:
- far silhouette;
- near trunk base and root anchoring;
- upward branch structure from the player eye;
- crown profile against sky;
- relationship to paths, roofs and neighboring trees.

Check leaf persistence and collision behavior in the target game after reload if tools allow. If the trunk is a stick, branches are untraceable or foliage is a sphere, rework its structure before adding flowering accents.
