# Visual reference research and image reading

Experienced Minecraft builders constantly observe photographs, architectural drawings and builds by other creators. MineShelt should teach agents to **extract construction principles from evidence**, not memorize a prefab or copy a screenshot block-for-block by accident.

## Source collection workflow

When the agent has browser/image search tools and the brief benefits from references:

1. Search **the target building role + biome + style + site condition** (“Minecraft coastal fishing workshop on cliffs”, not only “beautiful house”).
2. Collect at least three contrasting references when suitable:
   - an exemplary Minecraft building for massing/roof/craft;
   - a terrain/vegetation example for landform integration;
   - optionally a real-world architecture/landscape image for physical logic.
3. Prefer original creators' pages, official game tutorials and screenshots showing enough building context. Note edition, version and resource packs when supplied.
4. Capture structured notes on *what is actually visible*, not speculation about hidden interiors or unknown block IDs.
5. Synthesize a unique response to the user's site; do not clone the reference or lift someone else's downloadable structure into a mod without permission.
6. If image search is unavailable, use the brief and existing verified source material; clearly mark visual inference as unobserved.

## Visual analysis worksheet

For each source image, fill in a compact observation sheet:
- **Source, creator, URL, access date**;
- **View**: low eye level, aerial, orthographic, wide cinematic;
- **Main silhouette**: height/width impression, primary axis, distinct mass hierarchy;
- **Roof**: pitch, ridge direction, junctions, eaves, support;
- **Building depth**: wall datum vs projecting and recessed volume, real window reveals, support/underside, side/rear continuations; what cannot be seen from the screenshot;
- **Material palette**: broad *visual* warm/cool/value zones; block IDs only if positively known;
- **Gradients**: direction and reason; focal vs background;
- **Terrain**: how cut, fill, rock strata, river and planting behave;
- **Atmosphere**: lighting, shaders, season, perceived scale;
- **Limitations**: hidden elevations, possible post-processing, unknown mod/resource pack;
- **Transferable principle**: one design insight;
- **Do-not-copy note**: exact geometry or artist-specific motifs not to reuse.

### Photography pitfalls

Perspective, field of view, shaders, custom packs and color grading can make a very small build seem monumental. Seek multiple angles or a raw gameplay screenshot. Render shots do **not** prove playable, safe interiors. A single high-level cinematic shot may hide floating foundations or incomplete rear elevations.

If the screenshot uses a custom texture pack, don't silently assume vanilla blocks will match the visible palette.

## Better questions than “is it beautiful?”

- Can I sketch the silhouette in 10 seconds?
- Which elements genuinely support the roof/bridge?
- Which textures have a calm large area and which are broken up?
- Where does dirt or moss collect, and what causes it?
- Is the cliff a readable sequence of stone spines, shelves and talus?
- Are trees organized by crown shape and age or scattered as generic leaves?
- Where is the implied route from landscape to door?
- What is missing from this screenshot that I need to verify?

## Source selection for different briefs

- **Mountain fortress**: choose architecture, rock-cliff landscape and a real mountain geology reference. Identify *structural* reasons for retaining walls and route bends.
- **Taiga village**: choose one carpenter/cottage, one tree reference and one slope/path tutorial. Design settlement grammar, not cloned houses.
- **Japanese-inspired garden**: look for accurate landscaping/architecture and cultural context, distinguish real construction logic from superficial “zen” clichés.
- **Alien fantasy island**: take geology/ecology as a baseline and deliberately break its rules in a consistent, legible way.
- **Old town**: multiple elevations and street-level references matter more than one cinematic aerial render.

## Image-to-build translation

Never use pixel-to-block tracing as a default. Convert images into a **design grammar**:
- mass proportions → planned primary/secondary volumes;
- value hierarchy → wall/frame/roof swatches;
- terrain profile → chosen ridges, ledges, slopes and runoff;
- foliage distribution → authored tree and shrub placement zones;
- wear → physical exposure maps.

The specific build still needs original dimensions, interactions and construction decisions.

## Copyright and attribution

Treat creator screenshots, art, schematics, maps and videos as third-party content. Cite/link their original page with creator attribution. Do not mirror assets, reupload packs or redistribute adapted world files unless the creator grants appropriate permission. Respect license and site terms when programmatically downloading any image.

**A visual reference is evidence for study, not proof the agent built the depicted scene.**
