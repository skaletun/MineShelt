# Worked commission — a hand-authored harbor under a coastal cliff

This is a design study for **palette, geology, drainage, roads, vegetation and architecture together**. It is NOT a seed-based generated layout, actual placed world, tested schematic or universal coastal preset.

## Brief and observed/invented context

Client: “Create a fishing harbor and keeper's hall, not a flat spawn platform. It should look as if a professional Minecraft builder sculpted the shore by hand. Use a restrained block gradient. The small village must remain navigable.”

**Illustrative scene assumptions only:** shore approaches from the south, inland rises to the north, water level local Y=0, a rocky western spur protects a cove. The designer has not seen an actual game world; on a real task this section must be replaced by measured coordinates/screenshot observations.

Plan a local site approximately 62 blocks along X and 48 blocks along Z, with terrain rising to roughly Y=14 at the northern bluff. Exact profiles depend on the surveyed map. The **keeper's hall** is about 15×11 blocks (wall bounds, excluding wharf and roof), and one tall custom spruce frames the cliff. These are authored project choices rather than instructions to fill all 62×48 blocks.

## 1. Composition

Three large masses should read from sea:
1. low sheltered harbor water and curved quay;
2. midlevel hall with pitched roof and one modest chimney;
3. high rugged bluff with sparse trees and one broad rock ledge.

The cliff is the large background and should not visually swallow the hall. An open gap between hall roof and tree canopy protects the silhouette. A dock from the water approaches the landing and then ascends via a switchback route; the wharf has a functional loading area.

**Reject:** a giant sheer wall of stone with a flat platform welded to it, and a row of identical round boulders lining the water.

## 2. Author terrain sections

Choose three local cross sections to verify after surveying:

| Transect | Functional height profile (relative Y) | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West headland → harbor | bluff shelf ~10–14, steep rock face down to ~2, waterline 0 | exposed rock ribs and narrow toe scree |
| Northern hill → hall threshold | upland ~12, two terraces toward building plinth ~4–5 | retain mature grade; no artificial flat circle |
| Pier → keeper hall | water 0, quay ~2, landing route rises gently to hall at ~4–5 | step/ramp segments, safe cargo path |

The numbers communicate a design intent; in a real existing world they must be reconciled with measured surface Y at each site.

### Cliff sculpt

Trace two dominant rock spines, separated by a shallow erosion gully that flows toward the cove. Give spines different height and projection. Carve shelves where soil/trees can be rooted. Model a short talus field beneath the gully (stone core, cobblestone/gravel transition). Do **not** spray identical 3×3 stones uniformly across the face.

### Palette board

Create an actual in-world sample board if possible:

| Tested role | Candidate blocks | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| main cliff | stone, andesite | restrained cool mid-value rock mass |
| fractured ribs | tuff, cobblestone | only in ledges/crack sections |
| talus | cobblestone, gravel | lower cliff fallout and small slope |
| wooded soil | grass_block, podzol, coarse_dirt | soil pockets and high-footfall transitions |
| wharf/plinth | stone_bricks, cobblestone | worked stone vs rough erosion |
| hall frame | spruce_log and/or stripped_spruce_wood | warm readable timbers |
| infill | spruce_planks or lighter context-compatible material | calm contrast to stone bluff |
| roof | spruce_stairs/spruce_slabs; alternative dark-compatible roof family | one simple primary form |

These are **test candidates**, not claims of sampled visuals or definitive colors; verify states and exact IDs in the target runtime.

**Design decision:** Keep the background cliff mostly mid-valued and minimally textured near the hall silhouette. Reserve a darker/rougher stripe at a genuine fracture and lighter worn stone on a distant exposed ridge. Too many equally contrasting blocks would compete with the house.

## 3. Harbor's construction

Use a quay of worked masonry on load-bearing faces. The jetty extends from stable ground with a coherent rhythm of piers. A timber weather shelter covers one goods sorting spot. The hall contains a public chart/workroom, a keeper sleeping zone, a storage level reachable without clipping and visible sightline across the water.

A narrow stone stair behind the hall may ascend to the cliff shelf. Its route should match a realistic switchback rather than a floating staircase.

## 4. Hand-planned shoreline

At the exposed headland, emphasize rough rock at the waterline and eroded shelves. At the sheltered cove, create a small depositional sand/gravel pocket and shallow bank vegetation. Avoid making every water edge simultaneously sandy, mossy and cobbled. Water must visibly connect to sea; there are no random isolated 1-block puddles.

## 5. Planting and custom spruce

The custom spruce is placed where its silhouette frames the hall from the dock, not directly behind the chimney. Establish a rooted trunk, 2–3 purposeful main branch directions, uneven downward bough layers and modest empty spaces through the crown. Put smaller juveniles on inland soil pockets, sparing the wind-exposed rockface. Place shrubs low in sheltered niches and leave the cargo route clear.

## 6. Iterative expert corrections — proposed, not fabricated

**Potential failure A:** cliff and building both have highly variegated textures. **Repair:** reduce cliff contrast immediately behind the building and retest sea-level view.

**Potential failure B:** approach from wharf becomes an unnatural 5-block vertical rise. **Repair:** introduce a switchback, intermediate landing and hand-shaped earthwork rather than simply adding a ladder.

**Potential failure C:** giant spruce hides the hall at medium distance. **Repair:** recompose tree position/crown and preserve sky gap over roof; do not just chop random leaves.

**Potential failure D:** waterline shows a straight edge due to the quay ending. **Repair:** extend a coherent stone-to-natural-rock transition with a convincing break between built and eroded coastline.

## 7. Execution sequence

1. Inspect/record the actual site, world bounds and backups.
2. Mark waterline and main landforms; sculpt macro headland and sheltered cove.
3. Resolve keeper's hall datum, quay load path and path elevation.
4. Hand-edit cliff ribs, shelves, gully and talus before texturing.
5. Build hall frame and roof; review against cliff silhouette.
6. Construct pier, shelter, circulation and inhabitable rooms.
7. Swatch/test palette; apply **zone-led** wall and cliff transitions.
8. Place custom spruce, juveniles and ecologically justified groundcover.
9. Capture harbor hero view, cliff side, path section, underside of quay, hall rear/interior and night checks; revise based on actual defects.
10. Re-verify walkability, saved block states, water behavior and mod compatibility.

## 8. Proof standard

This document demonstrates **thought process and build-ready design intent**. No terrain, image, NBT or in-game artifact was created by writing this example. Do not reuse its numbers as if they describe an actual loaded world.
