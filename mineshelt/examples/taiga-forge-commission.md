# Worked commission — a taiga forge built by a real craftsperson

This is **one authored case study**, not a template to clone or an instruction to always build blacksmiths like this. Its purpose is to illustrate how MineShelt turns a brief into intentional choices.

## Client brief

“Create a working forge beside a rising taiga village path. The exterior should be striking but not fantastical. It must feel old, extended over time, habitable, and made of available materials. Build for a Minecraft Java mod and ensure that a player can navigate it.”

Assume the site has a one-block rise from south to north, a forest to the west and a busy path approaching from the south-east. In a real task these would come from surveying the game world rather than invention.

## 1. Design thesis

A low, sturdy stone-bottomed smithy with a long spruce gable, a broad smoke chimney near the rear work area, and a *later* low fuel/storage shed set into the east side. The public threshold is sheltered from snow by a small south porch.

Identity: **a toolmaker's workshop grown around its hearth**, not a fantasy tavern with a chimney stuck on.

## 2. Author the plan

Local axes: +X east, +Z south; ground datum is the southern grade Y0. The entire deliberately bounded architectural site is x=0..18, z=0..16, excluding path landscaping beyond it.

| Element | Approximate local bounds | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main workshop | x=1..13, z=1..12 | hottest/largest space and primary gable |
| East lean-to | x=14..18, z=4..12 | dry fuel and metal storage |
| South covered threshold | x=4..9, z=13..16 | shelter waiting customers and their tools |
| Furnace/hearth focus | around x=4..6, z=3..5 | smoke route directly into chimney |
| Main standing work area | around x=7..11, z=5..9 | clearance for anvils and access |
| Goods/public exchange zone | around x=7..11, z=10..12 | separates public from hot work |

This is a **reasoned spatial specification**. Before actual placement, adapt clearances and exact outlines to the real site and available interaction blocks. Avoid silently treating ranges as a verified imported structure.

## 3. Mass and section

Main volume dominates. The roof ridge runs approximately east–west (parallel to X), so the gables face east and west; its pitch is legible but restrained. Southern public porch hangs under the primary roof visual hierarchy. The east fuel extension has a lower single-slope roof, connecting beneath the main eave rather than slicing randomly through the ridge.

Give the main hall enough clear height for furnace work and a dramatic timber tie-beam. The chimney should project beyond the roof as a second, narrow vertical mass. Use its placement to explain why the western side of the building is visually heavier.

The 1-block northern elevation difference creates a stepped foundation and a short interior or outer grade correction, not a flattened 19×17 platform.

## 4. Palettes by function

- Fieldstone/cobblestone-like local stone at ground contact, forge base and chimney.
- Spruce logs or equivalent actual version-supported timber at corners, framing and structural roof members.
- Quiet timber/plaster-like infill where dry and protected, rather than a noisy mix on every wall.
- Dark weather-exposed roofing consistent with the roof shape; choose actual stair/slab options available.
- Limited iron/soot accents at hearth and tool display; warm lantern light at main door and working zone.

On the north and west faces, plausible splash and dampness sit low. Smoke staining travels up the chimney path. The east shed is newer wood with less weathering. No “random cracked block percentage”.

## 5. Front elevation and legibility

The porch makes the public entrance obvious from the approaching south-east path. A large opening near the working side admits daytime light; a smaller, protected opening serves storage. The gable remains a mostly quiet field with clear roof trim and modest ventilation, not twenty evenly spaced trapdoors.

From the distant path, the silhouette should show **main gable + chimney + low east extension**, readable before any tools or lanterns become visible.

## 6. Interior and small narrative evidence

Place the hearth, anvil and fuel access so a player can move around them. Set two distinct work surfaces, storage and a collection counter reachable from the public entrance. Add a single worn high-traffic floor strip from porch to exchange area and a separate work route toward fuel storage.

A modest drying rack, scrap pile and chopping block may occupy the shed edge. These are not randomly chosen assets: each supports an activity. Reserve empty space for the craftsperson to work.

## 7. Landscape

Keep the village path connected to the door by compacted material and two or three irregular but walkable contour changes. Place a covered stockpile close to the shed, not at an arbitrary corner. Retain forest trees that frame the silhouette; remove only ones that actually intersect the building footprint. Use coarse vegetation transition at the edge of cleared working ground.

## 8. Building and review order

1. Mark precise affected region and protect nearby world.
2. Build stepped footing and entry grade.
3. Build frame and dominant volumes.
4. Close gable and shed roof; inspect ridge and east connection.
5. Place functional chimney/hearth, doors/windows and check orientation.
6. Fit real work zones and verify walking and interactions.
7. Add site activity, paths, weathering and a restrained trim layer.
8. Take hero, side, rear, roof, interior and night images; change defects and retake comparable views.

## 9. Predicted failure checks — not fictional evidence

- Chimney may appear unsupported if stone transition begins only on roof: ensure continuity down to hearth base.
- South porch might collide with village path when snow rises: verify walkway clearance.
- Shed roof may terminate too high against the gable: check actual stair orientation and trim.
- Small plan may be cramped when workstation blocks have interactions: walk and measure.

This example intentionally ends without claiming the structure was built. A real MineShelt run should only say **built and inspected** after execution and actual in-game review.
